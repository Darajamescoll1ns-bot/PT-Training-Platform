The core idea

A good decision can lose. A bad decision can win. Variance hides which is which.

Poker and trading punish the same mistake: judging your choices by their results. A river suck-out and a lucky breakout both reward the wrong process — and a disciplined fold or a well-sized stop can look like a loss in the moment.

Edge over Variance separates the two. Every decision is scored against what a sound process would have done, so you can see your real edge underneath the noise of any single result.

How it works

Play a hand. Get graded. Think like a trader.

Every session is a loop: make real decisions under uncertainty, see exactly where your process held or broke, then carry the same reasoning straight into markets.

Play real hands

Sit down to heads-up or multiway seven-card stud and Texas hold'em against equity-driven bots. Antes, blinds, stacks and bet-sizing all behave like the real game — every street is a live decision.

Get graded street by street

A from-scratch hand evaluator and Monte-Carlo equity engine grade each choice the moment you make it — call, fold, size or raise — against what the math actually supported, with the pot-odds and EV shown.

Translate to markets

Each key decision is mapped to a trading analogue — position sizing, conviction, cutting a loser — with a plain-English lesson and a glossary so the instinct transfers to the desk.

The math behind every call

No black box. Show your work.

Each decision comes with the actual pot-odds and expected-value reasoning that graded it — the same arithmetic a disciplined player or trader runs in their head, made explicit so you can internalise it.

Equity is computed by a from-scratch seven-card evaluator and Monte-Carlo simulation. Where a true solver baseline isn't available off the shelf, approximations are clearly flagged — never silently assumed.

the math behind this decision
# 5th street — facing a half-pot bet
equity      = 0.61   # Monte-Carlo, vs live field
pot         = 12
to_call     = 6

# EV of calling = e·(pot) − (1−e)·(to_call)
ev_call  = 0.61*18 − 0.39*6
         = +8.64   → call is +EV

verdict  = "value-correct"
ev_loss  = 0.0      # no EV left on table
